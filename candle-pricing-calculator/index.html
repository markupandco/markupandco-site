How to price a candle in 2026

Most candle pricing advice is "materials times two" or "materials times three." That works until you sell at a market for a weekend and realize your weekend was free. The calculator above does it the way a business would: every input costed from what you actually paid, your time at a real hourly rate, a waste allowance because wax ends up on the counter, and then a price that leaves the margin you chose after the platform's cut.

The fragrance load math

Fragrance load is a percentage of the wax weight, not of the finished candle. So an 8 oz fill at an 8% load is not 8 oz of wax plus 0.64 oz of oil (that would overflow). It is 8 ÷ 1.08 = 7.41 oz of wax and 7.41 × 0.08 = 0.59 oz of fragrance oil. The calculator does this for you and gives batch totals to weigh out, with the waste allowance added so you don't come up one candle short.

Most soy and coconut-soy waxes are rated for 6–10%; paraffin blends often take 10–12%. Above the wax's maximum the oil doesn't bind and can pool or sweat, so check your supplier's number before pushing the load up for throw.

Four things that quietly eat candle profit

The jar. On an 8 oz candle the vessel is often the single biggest line, bigger than the wax. Buying by the case instead of the dozen typically cuts it 20–30%, and that drops straight to your margin.

Etsy's fee on shipping. Etsy charges its 6.5% transaction fee and 3% processing on the shipping you collect too, not just the item. If you charge $9 shipping on a $14 candle, about $0.86 of the shipping is fees. The calculator's Etsy price assumes free shipping or shipping priced separately; if you fold shipping into the item, price the shipping line with the same 9.5% on top.

Offsite Ads. If Etsy's Offsite Ads program brings a buyer, Etsy takes another 15% (12% once you pass $10k in the trailing year). You can't opt out below $10k. Either accept that some sales are thin or build 3–5% into every price to average it out.

Burn testing and failed batches. Every new wax, wick or fragrance costs a few test candles. Put something in the overhead line for them or they come out of your profit without you noticing.

Is 40% the right margin?

For direct sales, 40–60% is where most sustainable candle businesses land. Below 30%, one slow market or a price increase from your wax supplier puts you underwater. For wholesale, where the retailer roughly doubles your price, 25–35% on your side is normal, and the trade is volume and no fees. The calculator's wholesale line uses 25%.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to make a candle?

An 8 oz soy container candle usually costs $4–$7 all-in with mid-range supplies, about $3–$4.50 of that materials. Plug in your own prices above for an exact figure.

How do I calculate fragrance load?

Fragrance oil weight ÷ wax weight. To hit a fill weight: wax = fill ÷ (1 + load), fragrance = wax × load. The calculator does both and gives batch totals.

How do I price candles for a craft fair?

Use the direct-sale price and remember the booth fee is overhead: a $150 booth over 60 candles sold is $2.50 each. Card readers take about 2.6% + $0.10 per tap, which the direct price doesn't include.

How do I price candles for wholesale?

Retailers expect to buy at roughly half of what they'll sell for (keystone). Price wholesale from your cost at a 25–35% margin and check that doubling it lands at a believable retail price; if it doesn't, the product isn't a wholesale product yet.

Does this work for wax melts or soap?

Yes for anything poured by weight: set the fill weight to the clamshell or bar weight, use the mold or clamshell cost in the jar field, and set fragrance load to your recipe's. For cold process soap it prices the batch but doesn't calculate lye; use a lye calculator for that.
